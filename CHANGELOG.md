## Unreleased

- Exposure figures for a portfolio page (investing spec 2026-11-06, its section 5). New view `exposure_figures`,
  rebuilt by every `derive`: `top_three` and `sector_spread` and `ai_capex` on the brokerage, `stacked` and
  `crash_cost` on both Fidelity accounts rolled up, each with the `base` it is measured on, the plan's own
  `line_pct` (computed from the targets file, so it moves when the plan is revised; NULL for the two the rulebook
  draws no line for), `look_through_complete` with the measured `coverage_pct` for the two that read fund
  constituent weights, and a `note` in words. Built only from the configured accounts the warehouse records as
  Fidelity, so another broker's account in `[allocation.accounts]` cannot reach a figure. The plan's lines use all
  thirteen of the owner's names: `read_targets` now also returns the `planned` entries that carry a structured
  percentage, in their own `Targets.planned` - never in `entries`, which is what funds an account. Migration 0024.
- New view `trading_days`: NYSE trading days by rule (`check/calendar.py` fills `trading_days_raw`, so no day the
  calendar rejects can enter) that also have a `final = 1` price bar, with `final_bars`. It makes two things this
  repo already knew reachable from another repo over the read-only connection - the calendar, which was Python
  only, and the complete-close rule - so a consumer stops guessing a calendar from a price file and can never read
  a bar fetched before its close as a close. Also an index on `prices (date, final)`, which that join needs.
- Review fixes to those figures, all three in the same place - a number on a money page that claims more than it
  is. A fund holdings file that sums over 100% counts something twice, so `stacked` and `ai_capex` now publish no
  value there (only the measured coverage and the reason): a consumer draws an incomplete figure as "at least x",
  which would assert the opposite of what is wrong. A brokerage holding only cash published a true 0.0 against a
  real line - perfect diversification, measured on nothing - and now publishes nothing. Every note ends with the
  positions date its figure rests on, names any account whose snapshot is behind it, and says so when a run could
  not rebuild it and left the figure already published. `read_targets` reports a `planned` entry that carries a
  ticker and a target and then fails to read, instead of dropping it in silence, and a planned entry filed as
  `pct_of_account` reaches the plan it is planned for. The `trading_days` view seeks per day instead of
  aggregating the whole price table, which the consumer's own `ORDER BY day DESC LIMIT 30` could not use; both
  new raw tables are counted like every other derived table. The plan for this work lives in the owner's own
  investing repo, not here: it names holdings, and this repo is public.
- Fund look-through now joins share classes and foreign listings: `symbols.exposure_alias` (Alphabet's two classes
  as one GOOGL, eight foreign listings as the US lines they stand for, ROP.SW to RHHBY and never to Roper),
  applied in `read_look_through`, which sums weights that collide. `canonical()` is unchanged on purpose - it
  governs identity, and mapping GOOG onto GOOGL there would rewrite a real GOOG holding. A name's measured passive
  exposure can therefore be larger than before, which can move the weekly split's recommendation.
  `read_look_through_with_coverage` returns each fund's measured coverage (the sum of the weights as read, in
  percent, over 100 reported as measured rather than clamped), and `read_fund_sectors` reads the funds' published
  sector maps from the look-through folder's `fund_category_weights.json`.

- The week score (for the window's "This week" page; spec addendum 2026-10-31, the owner's picks). Each proposal now
  says whether this is the week its cash goes in: `score` 0-10 (a usual week starts at 7; cheap destinations add up to
  3 and premium ones take up to 6 by their share of the gap; an unexplained drop of a destination takes 1, at most 2),
  `verdict` go / half / hold from `[allocation] go_from` (7) and `half_from` (4), `fraction` 1 / 0.5 / 0 of the cash the
  split is made for (the rest waits), `parts` (what moved it, in plain words), `weeks_waited`, `forced` (`waited` after
  `max_wait_weeks`, 4; `under_and_cheap` when the largest-gap destinations making up half the gap are all cheap),
  `waiting_since` and `account_type`. A proposal that held money back is scored again once a week for the same cash.
  The `split` check also holds lines over cash x fraction and a fraction its verdict does not give. Migration 0023:
  the columns on `allocation_proposals_raw` and its view (the view only gains columns; older proposals read NULL).
- New views `holding_bands` (one overall band per holding of a configured account, as the split reads it, with its
  reasons and deciding score rows; a hold opened since on one of those blanks the band) and `allocation_targets` (each
  targets-file entry's `gap` either way with `side`, and `top`, by the split's own `split.entry_gap`), rebuilt by every
  split run, even when no proposal opens.

  TTM's period end, not with Yahoo's levered `freeCashflow` (that put every large company 70-1900% apart while the
  statements agree to the dollar). Like for like ("Purchase Of PPE") it may hold, and `p_fcf` with it; on Yahoo's wider
  "Capital Expenditure" line it only warns; quarters that do not reach our period end are not compared (info).

  8-K came after the close the drop ends on; the report is the next move's cause). Found on a live 4% drop the day of an

The sync also puts up its heartbeat before it opens the store, so `fdc check` waits for a sync that is still migrating. Found live: a check 8 s after the logon sync failed on 'duplicate column name'.
- Fund typing: an ETF that is a series of a trust (most ETFs) is not on SEC's company ticker map, so the `sec` step found no CIK and left it `unknown`: the `funds` step took no snapshots of it and it got no fund scores, and the weekly split charged it to caps as `type_unknown`. The step now also reads SEC's fund ticker list (`company_tickers_mf.json`, cached a week, asked only when a symbol of unknown type has no CIK): a symbol on it is typed `etf` (`mutual_fund` when five letters ending in X), its message "no CIK in SEC ticker map; on SEC's fund list: etf". A `[classify]` symbol is never retyped; when the list cannot be had the step goes on and says so. Already-stored ETFs are typed at the next sync.
- The weekly split: the `engine` step now also proposes how to share the cash waiting in each account across its holdings, from your own targets file. A proposal is written to the database, never an order: the split never sells, never moves money between accounts, never proposes more than the waiting cash, gives a `hold` name nothing, and works in whole dollars.
  - Settings: `[allocation]` in `config.toml` (`targets`, `fund_holdings`, `min_new` $25, `min_line` $10, `tilt` cheap 1.5 / usual 1.0 / premium 0.5, `household_days` 7) and `[allocation.accounts]` (targets-file account name = fdc account label); validated; the two paths are None until the file names them, so no owner file is read without a line (`fdc init`'s example names them). `derive/targets.py` reads the targets file (`read_targets`: percent as the file writes them, a 0.75x..1.25x band when an entry gives none, sleeves, caps, what it skips said in `problems`, only the file name kept) and the look-through folder (`read_look_through`: each `<FUND>.csv` as fractions).
  - Bands: `derive/split.py` `holding_band` (pure) gives a holding one band from its primary scores (a company's `pe`, `p_fcf`, `ev_ebit`, `ps`; a fund's `pe`, `dividend_yield`; each counted only when `ok` with a band): fewer than two is `usual`, more than half `premium` is `premium`, no `premium` and more than half `cheap` is `cheap` (`usual` when a company's free-cash-flow and operating margin changes are both known and both below zero; `quality_unknown` said when either is not known), anything else `usual` ("disagree"). It names every primary metric that did not count. Reason codes never hold a comma (a line's reasons are stored comma-joined): `cheap_by:pe+p_fcf`, and a comma in a sleeve's label becomes `+` in `sleeve_cut:`.
  - Plan: `plan_split` (pure) shares one account's cash across its entries: a percent entry's gap is its target of the account (positions plus the waiting cash) less what it holds, a direct/via-funds ratio entry's gap is the target times the household's dollars held through funds less its direct dollars; money goes by gap x the band's tilt and can grow past a target up to the band's top once others are full, never past a top, a sleeve's max or a cap (household-wide; a fund line counts at its look-through weight of the name, a fund with no file at the largest weight any file gives, said as `look_through_assumed`; missing look-through never makes room). Shares are floored to whole dollars, a line under `min_line` is folded into the largest line as far as its room allows, the rest waits for next week. A `hold` or misspelled rule, a duplicate, a ratio with no fund on file and a holding at or over its target get no line, each said in the notes. `household_of` builds the household (investment accounts only, kept when their newest positions are within `household_days` of the newest, each with its cash only when its cash row is that fresh; the notes count the accounts left out, never naming one). `models.SplitLine`.
  - Lifecycle: `split.run` reads the account's newest USD cash row: the first proposal needs `min_new`; with one open, `min_new` more cash makes a new proposal for all of it that replaces it, cash at least $1 lower closes it `spent` (placed in full or in part), anything between changes nothing; after a spent one, the next needs `min_new` above the lower of its closing cash and the lowest balance since. The same cash writes nothing. An account with no entries in the targets file, no cash row, a cash row older than its newest positions, or named twice on one fdc account gets no proposal; an open proposal it has, or one whose name left the config, stays open and the notes say so by name. No targets file, an unreadable one or no accounts: `skipped` with why, nothing touched. Lines carry their sources (the targets entry by file name and the account it is filed under, the score rows that decided the band, the tilt), the proposal its cash row and the targets file.
  - Storage: migration 0022 adds `allocation_proposals_raw` and `allocation_lines_raw` (a record kept like `check_findings`: a run only adds a proposal or closes one, an open proposal's lines are never rewritten; at most one open proposal per account) and the views `allocation_proposals` / `allocation_lines`, which show an open proposal `held` and blank its lines' dollars while a `split` hold on `proposal:<id>` is open. `models.AllocationProposal`; `Store.split_positions`, `split_cash`, `split_cash_low_since`, `split_scores`, `split_asset_types`, `open_proposal`, `open_proposal_keys`, `last_spent_proposal`, `write_proposal`, `close_proposal`, `check_split_rows`; `fdc status` counts both tables.
  - Step order: the `engine` step runs the engine, the re-check of the `scores` check alone, then `split.run` (only when the engine was not skipped and that re-check had no errors, else `; split: score re-check failed` and nothing is opened, replaced or closed), then the `split` check alone; its message ends `; split: N new, N replaced, N spent (notes: ...); split checks: H holds, W warnings, E errors` or `; split: <why skipped>` (the notes hold keys and counts only). `EngineReport.checked_at` is handed to the split.
  - Checker: a new check `split` (`check/split.py`, its own code and its own reading of the targets file, nothing from `derive/`) holds back an open proposal whose lines add up to more than the cash, a line that is not a whole number of dollars (at least 1), a line to a name the targets file marks `hold` in that account (or whose `rebalance` is not trades / contributions / hold) or that the account has no entry for (for `brokerage`, no `pct_of_brokerage` entry anywhere), a cash that is not a number, or no sources (not a list of records with a kind and a label). One finding per proposal (field `proposal:<id>`, every fault in its message); a closed proposal gets none; a missing or unreadable targets file is a warning ("cannot check hold names") while a proposal is open. `run_check` accepts a finding field matching `proposal:<id>`; `ENGINE_CHECK_NAMES` is now `scores`, `split`. The `scores` check also holds a fund's `ok` snapshot score no fund can have: P/E at or below 0 or above 200, yield above 0.25, expense ratio above 0.03 (`Store.check_score_rows` carries the asset type).
  - Caps across accounts: accounts are planned in sorted key order, and a new proposal counts every other account's open proposal lines (from earlier runs and earlier in the same run) as pending holdings (`with_pending`: a name directly, a fund through its look-through file, a fund without one at the assumed weight; the household total grows only by lines of an account whose cash is not already counted), so two accounts cannot together pass a household cap. A proposal being replaced does not count its own old lines.
  - Unknown types: a symbol with a look-through file is a fund (its band from the fund metrics, its caps through its file) even when `securities.asset_type` is unknown or missing; any other symbol of unknown or missing type is, for caps, a fund without a file (the assumed weight), and its line says `type_unknown`.
  - A name with any `hold` entry in the account is `hold`, even after a duplicate entry that says `contributions`. When no line reaches `min_line`, everything the fill placed is folded into the largest share (ties alphabetical) within its room (its top, every sleeve and cap over it), and waits only if that is still under `min_line`.
  - `placed` is the whole dollars the lines place (cash = `placed` + `waits` to the cent), not the number of lines.
  - A placement a deposit hid: an open proposal is also `spent` when the account has a `buy` of a holding (not a money market fund) dated after its `cash_date` and on or before the newest cash row's date (`Store.split_buys_between`); a new proposal for all of the cash then opens in the same run when it is at least `min_new`. When the transactions feed lags, the net-cash rules still apply.

  - Bad numbers: an integer too large for a float is no number in both targets readers (an entry skipped with a problem, never an OverflowError), and JSON nested too deeply is "the targets file is nested too deeply to read" (the split skips; the checker warns). If `split.run` raises, the engine step still runs the `split` re-check on what it wrote, then reports the error (status `error`, the engine and check counts kept).

- `funds` step (after `sec`): a daily valuation snapshot per fund (ETF, mutual fund) from Yahoo into `fund_snapshots` (trailing and forward P/E, dividend yield, expense ratio as fractions; migration 0015), one Yahoo call per fund per New York day. A fund with no P/E or yield of its own can borrow an ETF's with `[fund_proxies]` in `config.toml`; the row's `source` says so. History starts the day it first runs. Yahoo is reached through a `YahooClient` seam (`collectors/yahoo.py`) that tests replace. yfinance logs a failed request instead of raising (its `hide_exceptions`): an error it logs while asking fails the ask, so the fund is asked again next run, and a fund that comes back with no figures after a day it had a snapshot is not marked either. A yield or expense ratio out of range, or whose two Yahoo readings (`yield` against `dividendYield`, `netExpenseRatio` against `annualReportExpenseRatio`) are 10 times or more apart, is refused with a note. A run with no fund to ask is `skipped`, which the exit code leaves out as it does the earnings step's.
- Benchmarks: a sync collects prices for `SPY` and the eleven sector SPDRs (`[benchmarks]` in `config.toml` changes them), marks them in `securities.benchmark` (migration 0014), keeps them out of `fdc export` unless held or listed, never parks them (one parked before it became a benchmark is unparked), and the checker's `prices2` compares their newest close with a second provider's.
- `fdc check --summary` lists every open finding of the checker, holds first, without running a check; `fdc status` and the console's steps table show when the checker last ran and how many holds and warnings are open; `scripts/schedule_check.ps1` registers a daily 07:00 `fdc check` task as a backstop for a day the sync did not run.
- Known: SEC's companyfacts answer can lag a filing by months. A large-cap's 10-Q filed in July was absent from
  SEC's companyfacts answer three months later (no fact carried its accession number and no period ended after
  March), although SEC's submissions list showed the filing with XBRL. fdc stores every fact companyfacts returns
  (the stored rows matched the answer one for one), so its TTM stopped at the March quarter. Nothing in fdc dropped
  it and no code changed; the checker's filings check (SEC's submissions list against `sec_facts`) holds such a
  company's TTM-based values from the filing date until the facts appear.
- `macro` step (after `prices`): FRED economic numbers into `macro_observations` (CPI, core CPI, the Fed's target range, the 10-year yield, unemployment) with a free `FRED_API_KEY` in `.env`; skipped without it. Migration 0010.
- `fdc console`: a live terminal screen (Textual) with three tabs: each step's last result and staleness plus
  the scheduled task's registration (Windows), every source and output with its last activity, and the history
  of runs with who started them and each step's message. `r` runs the sync, `s` one step, `ctrl+p` the
  palette; `fdc console --once` prints the same content once. The console only reads, over a read-only
  connection (`readonly.open_store`; the universe it counts comes from `universe.universe_symbols`, which writes
  nothing); a run it starts is an `fdc sync` subprocess that carries on after quitting. Migration 0009 indexes
  `sec_facts(filed)` so the Sources tab's newest-filing lookup is instant.
- The console is easier to read and move around in: every tab is a table with a detail strip under it that
  shows the whole highlighted row (full messages, full paths), tables show short text and numbers with
  separators, the status band is colored by state and names the next scheduled run, Sources leads with the
  state and shows short paths, Runs covers the last seven days with the date once per day and dims `unknown`
  origins, the schedule sits in its own panel, `left`/`right` switch tabs, `tab` moves between a table and
  its strip, and `?` opens a help screen.
- `scripts\open_console.ps1` opens the live screen in a PowerShell window of its own and returns, so a shortcut or
  another program can launch the console; the window stays open with the exit code if the console fails.
- A running sync keeps a heartbeat (`data/sync.heartbeat.json`: run, step, progress line, pid, origin) and
  removes it when it ends; a sync that finds another alive waits its turn (up to 30 minutes). Every run records
  its `origin` (migration 0008: `scheduled`, `manual`, `console`; older rows read `unknown`), and
  `scripts/run_sync.ps1` passes `--origin scheduled`.
- `derive` writes `unexplained_daily` (migration 0007): for each account and day, the change in the replay that
  no transaction or split explains, as its cash part, its shares part at the day's close, and their sum. Most often
  this is a deposit the broker's balance shows before its activity feed posts it. A consumer that took money moved
  only from `transactions` read such a deposit as growth. An ordinary day has no row, nor does a change under a
  dollar. Changes that cancel within a week (a value wrong for a day or two, such as a money-market sweep counted
  twice) moved no money and have no row either. When the feed dates a deposit
  after the balance showed it, the replay now pairs it with that earlier cash instead of adding it again as money in
  transit. A split the records carry as a transaction (Fidelity's share distribution) isn't counted twice.
  `Store.splits_by_symbol()` feeds the replay its splits.
- The scheduled sync (`scripts/schedule_sync.ps1`) runs on battery, and a start that arrives while a sync
  is running waits its turn instead of being dropped. A laptop left unplugged never synced, and a sync that
  began just before a feed finished writing left the warehouse a day behind.
- Connections: `fdc connect snaptrade` (brokerages, a personal key) and `fdc connect simplefin` (banks, cards
  and loans, balances only), `fdc connections`, `fdc disconnect`, `fdc accounts` and `fdc accounts set`. Keys
  live in the operating system's key store, or in `.env` on a computer without one. `fdc sync` gains a first
  step, `connections`. Migration 0006 adds the `connections` table, an account's `external_key`, `origin`,
  `kind_confirmed`, `credit_limit`, `rate_pct` and `flows`, and `cash_balances.available`. A connected account
  is known by a hashed key, so a rename keeps its history. Spec: `docs/superpowers/specs/2026-09-29-connections-design.md`.
- Identical transactions in one import are kept as separate rows (three $100 deposits on the same day
  were stored as one). The first keeps its old dedupe key, so re-importing a file still changes nothing.
- The replay no longer decides by the clock alone whether a snapshot already holds its day's rows. A
  statement's own units say which same-day trades it holds, and its cash says which deposits and other cash
  rows have landed; the fetch time only breaks ties. Fixes a buy counted twice when a feed stamped
  start-of-day was live, a deposit dropped until the balance showed it (a cash-only statement has no fetch
  time), and an instant deposit added on top of a morning statement that already held it. A deposit on its
  way counts in the account from its date; one the statements never show stops counting after seven days.
- Fidelity history: IRA deposits ("CASH CONTRIBUTION CURRENT YEAR" / "PRIOR YEAR") are contributions, not
  'other', so they count as money put in; "DIRECT DEBIT" is a withdrawal; a share distribution (a split) moves
  shares and no cash. Migration 0005 repairs rows already stored: retypes them, drops the IRA deposits the
  SnapTrade feed already holds, stores 0 units as NULL, and re-keys each row the way an import keys it now.
  A history file imported before the identical-rows fix can be imported again to recover a collapsed repeat.
